## Mentor's Instructions

**Objective:**
The primary goal of this seminar is to gain a profound understanding of the core research paper (along with the two additional papers you have selected) and to successfully explain these concepts to others through both a written thesis and an oral presentation. You should assume that your audience has a general background in Computer Science but lacks specific knowledge of the particular papers you are studying.

**1. Written Thesis:**
You are required to prepare a written summary of the material (spanning several dozen pages), focusing first and foremost on the conceptual level.
* **Deep Understanding:** This is not a "dry" summary; your writing must demonstrate that you have fully internalized and deeply understood the concepts.
* **Originality:** Where appropriate, you should consider providing your own examples and adding explanations beyond what is found in the original source material.
* **Autonomy:** You have full control over how to organize the material, which examples to select, how to demonstrate concepts, and how to prove relevant results. All decisions regarding emphasis and depth are yours.
* **Curation:** Please note that the volume of material may be too large to include entirely in the written work or the presentation. Deciding how to curate the content—what to emphasize and what to omit—is a core part of the assignment.
* **Bibliography:** You must select two papers from the bibliography of your main paper, study them, and integrate them into your work and presentation. These can serve as background for methods not detailed in the main paper, alternative approaches, historical context, etc.

**2. Presentation:**
You will deliver a 45-minute lecture explaining your work orally, supported by a suitable slide deck.
* **Slide Design:** Significant emphasis should be placed on the quality of the slides. They are not the written work; do not "copy-paste" from your thesis.
* **Text Minimalization:** Avoid long blocks of text on the slides, especially information you are already delivering orally.
* **Visuals:** Use visual illustrations as much as possible to help the audience understand your oral explanations.
* **Sequential Disclosure:** If you must present long texts, display them line-by-line, discussing each point as it appears. However, it is highly recommended to keep text to a minimum.

**3. Progress Meetings:**
We will meet three times via Zoom:
* **First Meeting:** Prepare a presentation covering the first half of the material (with slides).
* **Second Meeting:** Prepare a presentation covering the second half (with slides).
* **Third Meeting:** A full rehearsal of the entire lecture in preparation for your final exam.
* **Feedback:** Alongside each meeting, send me your progress on the written work. During each session, we will discuss the material and the lecture, and I will provide a written summary of my comments and feedback.

**4. Final Exam:**
Once I determine you are sufficiently prepared following the third meeting, we will schedule your exam presentation. This will take place before the Seminar Coordinator, myself, and additional representatives from the department.

**Grading:**
Your final grade will be determined by your written thesis, your performance during the exam presentation, and my overall impression of your work throughout the process.
